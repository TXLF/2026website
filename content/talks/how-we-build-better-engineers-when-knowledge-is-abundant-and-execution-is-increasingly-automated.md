Now we can generate thousands of lines of code in seconds, yet much of the conversation around AI has focused on prompts, agents, and productivity. We're optimizing for speed while asking the wrong question.


As information becomes abundant and execution increasingly automated, what does it actually mean to become an excellent engineer?

This talk explores how AI is reshaping the skills that matter most—not by replacing engineering fundamentals, but by elevating them. As tools continue to evolve, engineering principles remain our foundation. Judgment, systems thinking, curiosity, mentorship, collaboration, and stewardship are becoming the qualities that distinguish exceptional engineers, and they're the skills we'll need to intentionally cultivate in ourselves and the next generation.

Drawing on lessons from open source, Linux engineering, and real-world AI workflows, we'll explore practical ways to use AI as a learning partner rather than a thinking replacement. We'll cover techniques such as creating effective AI context, reviewing AI-generated work, building project memory, and using AI to deepen understanding instead of bypassing it.

Attendees will leave with practical engineering techniques they can apply immediately, a framework for building long-term engineering judgment, and a renewed perspective on how to invest in themselves, mentor others, and help shape the future of our profession.
